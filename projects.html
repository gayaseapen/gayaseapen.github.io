Myrtle Beach Motorcycle Project (2024- Ongoing)

A collaborative project that brings together artists, researchers, students, and members of the motorcycling community in Myrtle Beach and the broader American South to understand and celebrate the history and culture of motorcycling. This public facing project aimed to grasp the complexities of the history of motorycling, its encounters with law enforcements, indivisual and communal expression using sound and automobiles. A ground of student researches and I carrying out interviews and visiting biking related events and festivals in the Myrtle Beach and Atlantic Beach area. The project led to an exhibition and public event at the Rebbeca Randall Bryan Art Gallery in September 2025 that brought the work of photographers Armon Means, Jade Sweeting, and late Dr. John Howie. A short-trailer film (on the right) that I conceptualized and produced with student researchers was also featured in this exhibition. "As summer rolls in, the cruisers—an affectionate term for rumbling Harley Riders—start to take over the roads. You can spot them in every shape and size, often with country music blaring from their speakers. From behind a car window, it almost feels like they're the only ones truly enjoying the sunshine. Every now and then, a dual-sport bike zips through city traffic. Even though dual sports aren't as flashy as cruisers, their spunk is still impossible to ignore. Motorcycling in Myrtle Beach has a long legacy, going back almost a century. Nationally, biking clubs became popular as soldiers returned from the First World War. The connection between motorcycles and military life has evolved into a longing for freedom, style, and community."

Living Séance at the Beach: A Visual Poem






A visual poem that I produced along with faculty and students in Master's in Writing program and Digital Culture and Design. The shoot and recording sessions for this project were carried out along the beach and swamp landscapes in the Huntinton State Parks. The team and I conceptualized visuals to explore themes that emerged in the poem: memory, pain, and time. A creative back and forth ensued; we looked for visual resemblences between the metaphors that came from nature but resonated with the speaker. Camera, sound mixing, color grading, studio recording, and lighting emerged as means to enhance the feeling that emerged from the poem.
